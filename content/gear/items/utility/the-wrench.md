*The service port is inside the casing. You brought your own way in.*

A directional electromagnetic radiation module attached to a slate or deck. The Wrench couples a controlled signal into a target's internal electronics, using microwave emissions to reach circuitry that has no active network connection. Maintenance crews use it to reach sealed controllers. Intrusion specialists point it at someone else's equipment.

**Effect:** Provides an access path for [hacking actions](/core-rulebook/hacking-and-cyber-warfare/) against electronic equipment, including weapons, IFF systems, and frame controllers. Bring the emitter within 5 cm of the target casing. Its intended use is manipulating integrated circuits without opening the enclosure. Intervening material and shielding can still prevent the signal from reaching the circuitry; the range is a maximum, not guaranteed penetration. Closing the distance or exposing the electronics can make an otherwise blocked attempt possible.

Declare the function you intend to affect and resolve the intrusion with the normal prerequisites, Action Pool, TN, and program rules. The module supplies no software tier bonus or automatic control. A successful intrusion affects the declared equipment functions; it does not control the wearer or rewrite their orders. Purely mechanical functions have no circuitry for it to address.

**Binding rationale:** Maintaining signal coupling and separating the target's responses from interference takes continuous attention. This hardware cost is separate from active software costs; software-only Load Bearing does not cover it. A Neural Link's systems allowance can cover it when operated through that link.
