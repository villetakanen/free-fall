# The CSA Marines

**GM reference — draft opposition and scenario-local equipment.** Four marines arrive in [The Second Boarding](/scenarios/northern-lights/the-second-boarding/): two Hardshell-equipped assaulters, one hacker, and one sniper. Weapon profiles, Attribute reserves, and final custom gear statistics remain to be set.

## Friday-night cleanup

For the marines, this is Friday-night cleanup. Breach the transport, shoot the amateurs, secure the cargo. Beers afterward. Their equipment is expensive, their intelligence is authoritative, and the people aboard are already classified as targets. They expect a successful operation and a few stories to tell.

Play their confidence as casual: clipped jokes, routine target calls, impatience with resistance. They know their drills. They have not prepared emotionally for those drills to fail. Hubris is their starting condition.

When the assault turns against them, let that confidence crack. A marine freezes, fires too much, demands support, or backs away from an uncleared doorway. The commander may restore order—or make things worse by insisting the picture must be wrong. Training gives them responses; it does not make them fearless. Choose reactions from what each person experiences; panic is possible, not a compulsory scripted turn. Their contempt does not remove the EU citizen capture exception.

## Superiority by support

The CSA sends four people with an institution behind them. Logistics, intelligence, identity records, access credentials, and equipment support arrive packaged into their gear. Quiet approach, violent entry, coordinated advance: the marines expect their equipment to settle routine questions before a human has to ask them.

They enter with prepared identity records, assigned lanes, and a common plan. During the fight they report targets, positions, and cleared compartments by voice over internal laser-point-comms (LPC). The hacker commands the team and coordinates those reports; the sniper calls observations. Each marine’s equipment processes its own local sensor picture. Their advantage is integration, not a claim that every individual weapon or frame is better than the contractors' best hardware.

That support does not require instantaneous communication with distant headquarters. Prepared records and credentials travel with the team. Their LPC carries analog voice only. Separate local data links carry selected sensor views and identification data to the commander; they do not require a connection to distant headquarters.

## The hacker commands

The person with the access credentials gives the orders. CSA puts technical access and institutional clearance in the same hands: the hacker is this team’s commander. The assaulters clear the way while the commander follows from protected positions, watches their feeds, and directs the operation. The sniper covers usable sightlines. Keep the commander close enough to maintain local links and move with the team; cover and available connections determine their position, not a fixed marching order.

**Remote operations, local command.** The commander deploys with the assault because headquarters is too far away to direct a close fight. They can observe through a teammate’s sensors without standing beside them. Track which feeds reach the commander and what those sensors can actually see. A camera does not see through bulkheads; an interrupted feed supplies no fresh observations. Spoken reports over LPC can fill gaps, with the limits of what the reporting marine knows.

The commander’s systems work still consumes actions. Directing the team does not grant extra attacks, simultaneous hacks, or control of another marine’s decisions.

## Analog command, hackable equipment

The team operates under sealed CSA orders without an external command uplink. They seize the Whisper's suspected contraband, eliminate witnesses, and recover the hardware. They do not know what Origin-17 is. Vantage's EU Class A citizenship creates the established capture exception.

**The marines carry their orders; their machines do not command them.** They do not negotiate or maintain any radio channel to the contractors. Hails receive no response. Valid THESEUS credentials have no authority to replace their CSA mission. Hacking a display cannot rewrite their loyalties or turn them into obedient agents. They may retreat or rout when the assault becomes untenable; that is a response to the fight, not an invitation to bargain.

“Analog” describes this command discipline. Their weapons, frames, sensors, IFF units, and comm hardware remain electronic and hackable. IFF is a dumb identification and coordination system, not an intelligence deciding whom they must obey. A marine can switch it off, losing its support and both combat bonuses below. Switching it back on does not bypass the established duration of a successful Spike or Blast.

**No active network connection is not immunity.** In this setting, suitable electromagnetic radiation (EMR) tools can couple into internal circuitry—for example, using microwaves to reach a device's internal cards and attempt a remote override. Establish the tool, target, range, shielding, and intended function under [Hacking & Cyber Warfare](/core-rulebook/hacking-and-cyber-warfare/). This supplies another possible access path, not automatic success or unlimited reach. Origin-17's offered support retains its own connection and sight requirements.

Target the equipment's functions: disable a weapon's electronics, interfere with a frame, or disrupt IFF. Control of equipment can endanger or constrain its wearer. It does not grant control of the person.

### Laser-point-comms

Internal LPC is analog voice only. Treat the links as effectively unhackable in transit: access to the conversation requires a successful **Pwn of a marine’s comm unit**, with the listening or voice-injection function declared. The endpoint remains vulnerable through a feasible equipment-access path, including The Wrench at its contact-distance range. Pwn gives only the declared access and duration. It does not open the separate sensor-feed links, propagate programs to teammates, or make a spoken instruction authoritative. Those data links are separate hacking opportunities: establish the transmitting endpoint, receiving endpoint, channel and intended function. Access to one feed does not grant every connected device or the commander’s clearance.

## Four jobs

| Marine | Equipment direction | Task |
|---|---|---|
| Assault operator ×2 | Hardshell Combat Exoskeleton with CSA IFF/COMMS and boarding weapons | Secure the breach and advance toward the lab using shared lanes and target information. |
| Hacker / commander | Custom intrusion kit, command feeds and defensive software | Direct the assault from cover, maintain the combat picture, contest ship systems, and investigate hostile interference. |
| Sniper | Custom marksman weapon and targeting system | Observe exposed routes, feed target information to the team, and cover their movement from a suitable firing position. |

Only the two assaulters are committed to the immediate push through the interior. The hacker's systems work consumes actions; the sniper needs a real sightline and must reposition when the crew denies it. Their roles create simultaneous pressures without giving all four an automatic shot into every compartment.

## CSA assault frame — design basis

Use the catalog **Hardshell Combat Exoskeleton** for both assaulters: FRM 24, SYS 6, AV 4, Hardened, EVA thrusters, magnetic boots and eight hours of life support. Each has two Assault hardpoints and one Support hardpoint. Pilot Binding is Ghost 3; the active Combat Targeting Suite adds Ghost 1. Use the catalog’s remaining qualities unchanged.

The **CSA IFF/COMMS package occupies the Support hardpoint** on each frame. It provides the identification and targeting support, analog LPC voice and separate command feeds described below. Target those functions individually: suppressing IFF removes its combat bonuses without automatically silencing communications. Physical destruction of the whole package can remove all of its functions; resolve the actual damage and scope.

The two Assault mounts remain to be finalized. The proposed heavy rifles, lead operator’s plasma lance and second operator’s shotgun are not yet an approved loadout. Complete weapon compatibility, targeting benefits and pilot reserves before running the fight; do not stack an unspecified catalog targeting bonus on top of the defined CSA IFF bonus.

## Local systems and coordination

**IFF and identity checks.** Their identification-friend-or-foe systems distinguish teammates and classify other contacts, including civilians, contractors, and protected persons. [Vantage knows his Class A citizenship affords commercial protection](/scenarios/northern-lights/c-02-the-operator/#eu-full-citizen-class-a). The following orders are GM information, not a surprise about his own status.

**The one they take alive.** Harming a recognised EU Class A citizen carries commercial consequences. The squad’s no-witnesses orders therefore carve Vantage out: capture him, or leave him recoverable through medical and cybernetic intervention. This restriction constrains their target selection and use of force; it grants him no AV or physical immunity. The player can deliberately invoke a protection Vantage already understands. Do not require a later revelation before allowing that choice.

Before operative classification, prepared records and compliant targeting overlays can identify him. Other credible identity evidence can establish the same fact. Track which marines recognise him and what they have communicated through feeds or voice. Disabling IFF removes its combat bonuses and automated identification support; it does not erase an identification already made, revoke citizenship, or replace the marines’ orders. An uncertain identity needs verification, not automatic permission to ignore a known protected person.

### Classified operative identities

If Origin-17 has elevated a contractor, personal identity fields return **CLASSIFIED — N/A**. The identity is access-restricted, not deleted. **Only the CSA marine commander has sufficient clearance to read the THESEUS OPSEC designation behind that result.** The hacker-commander can check a contact through identification data relayed from a teammate; a face-to-face encounter is unnecessary. The relevant identification and record-access path must be available. Other marines do not gain the same access from their local IFF, from carrying the feed, or from seeing a valid signature on a directive.

Distinguish what a marine knows from what their display can currently show. Classification does not erase a previous recognition, revoke Vantage’s citizenship, or remove its Binding. If Vantage is the elevated contractor and has not yet been recognised, ordinary classified records cannot establish his citizenship; identification needs other credible evidence. Knowledge already established can still be communicated by voice.

Reading an operative designation supplies information. It does not itself replace sealed CSA orders, make the marines obey the contractor, or guarantee escape. The commander continues the sealed mission with that knowledge; THESEUS status supplies no command override. The EU citizenship capture exception remains separate.

**Shared targeting.** One marine’s spoken observation can inform the others through LPC; the commander can also use a connected sensor feed. A target handoff gives another operator information; it does not grant sight through a bulkhead, a free attack, or an automatic hit. Mark who has actually seen the target and who is relying on a report.

**Access credentials.** Military credentials and requisition authority give the hacker ways to approach compatible commercial systems. Establish whether a system recognizes that authority. Credentials can supply a legitimate access path; they are not universal control of every hatch, gun, or machine aboard the Whisper.

**Local diagnostics.** Each marine sees their own injuries, ammunition state, and frame faults. Teammates learn through spoken reports or direct observation; a sensor feed shows only what it actually captures, not an automatic readout of every reserve or fault. They can change lanes, cover an affected operator, or send assistance once they know. Each response still takes the actions it requires.

## Give the hackers something to touch

### IFF combat bonuses

While a marine's local IFF is functioning, apply **+5 to each die on their attack rolls** and **+5 to the TN of attacks against them**. The defensive benefit raises the attacker's TN; it is not an extra defense roll or AV. Losing IFF removes both bonuses while leaving the marine's ordinary equipment and training intact.

Show the assistance: coordinated changes of position, matching target calls, and warnings that let a marine move before a shot. When a hack takes it away, make the loss just as apparent.

### Spike or Blast the IFF

Declare the affected target or group and establish access before rolling under [Hacking & Cyber Warfare](/core-rulebook/hacking-and-cyber-warfare/). These are the scenario's specific effects on a successful intrusion:

| Program | Effect on local IFF |
|---|---|
| **Spike** | Permanently disable one target's IFF for this encounter. Both bonuses are lost. A reboot or an action from the marine hacker cannot restore it during the fight. |
| **Blast** | Suppress IFF for the declared, reachable group immediately and through the end of each affected marine's next turn. Both bonuses are absent throughout that interval, including against attacks made while the marine is not acting. IFF returns afterward unless permanently disabled or suppressed again. |

A further successful Blast renews suppression through the affected targets' next turns; durations do not accumulate into a bank of future turns. A marine whose IFF was disabled by Spike does not recover when a Blast expires.

Repeated Blasts are a valid way to keep the team's advantage down. BLACK adds +15 per assigned die: against TN 16 or lower every die meets the TN, while higher TNs can still produce failures. A natural 1 still compromises the software after resolution. Origin-17 can supply a fresh program for every allocated attack, including multiple attacks in the same turn. The player uses their normal Action Pool dice and pays no upfront personal software Binding, but **each natural 1 adds permanent Ghost 1 Binding to The Singularity only while below its Ghost 3 cap and within the host’s capacity; otherwise it causes 1 level of Compromise Harm**, as explained in the private handout.

That makes the choice concrete: **Spike removes one target's advantage for good; Blast suppresses several while the player keeps committing actions to it.** Respect the support's sight requirement and the declared reach of the Blast; a visible assaulter does not automatically expose an unseen sniper elsewhere. Do not invent escalating immunity or an instant hacker reset to cancel a successful intrusion.

### Other interventions

These systems expose bounded functions for normal hacking resolution and the [BLACK support offered by Origin-17](/scenarios/northern-lights/the-read/). Declare the specific target, reachable interface, and intended effect before rolling. Possible interventions include:

- Mark a cleared corridor as uncleared on one marine’s local display, forcing that operator to question the route.
- Pwn a comm unit to interfere with a spoken target handoff, forcing the recipient to acquire the target independently.
- Report a local frame fault, drawing attention toward a problem the operator must verify.
- Corrupt a local identity assessment, creating a conflict with another sensor or record.

Choose the program type appropriate to the effect. These are opportunities, not automatic outcomes: assess reach, scope, and defenses under [Hacking & Cyber Warfare](/core-rulebook/hacking-and-cyber-warfare/). A successful local alteration affects that function. It does not automatically rewrite every teammate's records, make a marine shoot a friend, or disable the entire squad.

Show the consequence in play. A marine checks a doorway, requests a second identification, breaks off a handoff, or spends attention verifying their suit. A useful hack buys a concrete opening. Do not erase it immediately with a claim that the CSA has better computers.

## When the picture breaks

A severed feed leaves the commander with the last received picture. They must seek a report, restore a usable connection, reposition, or act on incomplete information. The assaulters still have their orders, but must decide what to do with an incomplete picture. Loss of a command feed does not by itself disable their local IFF or erase earlier identifications. Their commander's authority survives the outage; immediate visibility does not.

Direct observation, voice communication and checking one another are available responses, not a guaranteed disciplined fallback. Initial irritation and demands for a fix can become hesitation or panic when impossible readings, failed weapons or a wounded teammate overturn their expectations. One operator may try a familiar drill while another freezes, fires excessively or retreats. The commander can demand repeated confirmation, blame an operator or issue orders from an obsolete picture. Verifying information or restoring coordination takes attention and appropriate actions. This fallback does not restore the IFF bonuses: Spike lasts for the encounter, and Blast follows the duration above. If the commander holds their nerve, they can address other interference or pursue its source. They share the same action limits and uncertainty as the rest of the team; panicked fire grants no extra attacks.

Make the cracks visible: an unanswered demand for support, conflicting calls, a stalled advance, a marine backing toward cover. Let the crew exploit those openings. Neither automatic recovery nor automatic rout should cancel what actually happens in the fight.

Retreat and rout are possible; negotiation is not. The exact withdrawal triggers and escape procedure remain to be established. Before finalizing the encounter, set those alongside the four loadouts, software defenses, and Attribute reserves. Keep [Vantage's premium hardware](/scenarios/northern-lights/c-02-the-operator/) and [Stitch's imposing but lightly armed Havoc](/scenarios/northern-lights/c-04-the-fixer/) relevant when choosing those values.

**Target access for BLACK support:** sight and a live support connection remain required, but neither replaces a suitable path from your equipment to the target. Establish the transmitting interface, receiving device and channel. The supplied program does not give you O17’s prepared module hardware or universal implant access.
