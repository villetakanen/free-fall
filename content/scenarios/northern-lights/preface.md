## What This Is

*Northern Lights* is written as a homage to the classic Cyberpunk 2020 mini-scenarios: brutal, funny, and often quite dark. The characters are not heroes. Nobody in this story is. But they are, absolutely and unapologetically, **the stars of the show** — the camera loves them, the complications orbit them, and when things go wrong, they go wrong *cinematically*.

Run it in that spirit. Reward audacity. Let bad plans fail loudly and good plans fail interestingly. Keep the pacing tight and the humor pitch-black. When in doubt, cut to the thing that hurts, then let somebody crack a joke over comms.

## The Shape of the Job

On paper, this is the cleanest contract the crew will ever accept — and they accepted it the way they accept everything: off the platform, with a thumbprint.

The crew is a registered contract unit on a public job market — the cynical far end of the gig economy, where wetwork posts beside courier runs and a smart contract escrows the pay before anyone breaches a hull. The client is a very-high-clearance operator — **PROTOCOL BLACK**, no name, no face, just a clearance tier the platform is legally required not to look behind — offering phenomenal money for a simple wetwork-and-salvage gig. The parameters are unusually good: solid intel, a clean intercept solution, a defined target. Board a silent transport, eliminate the civilian crew *while suited* (the posting is specific: the ship stays in vacuum, no witnesses, no survivors), and recover the QNA’s physical storage. Purge the array. Get paid. Retire a little. (The vessel that makes them eligible for work like this is [The Vessel](/scenarios/northern-lights/the-vessel/).)

## The deathtrap (GM)

The briefing offers a clean job because it describes the wrong situation. Do not prepare a guaranteed route to delivery, payment and a safe return.

- **It is not one QNA.** The manifest declares an advanced laboratory module with its own fusion power and substantial computing hardware. The actual target is an active NHE supported by multiple QNAs and other processing equipment. Removing or purging the named array is not automatically equivalent to disabling that intelligence’s entire local embodiment.
- **The CSA team is already coming.** Its doctrine is lethal to those aboard, with the established capture exception for the recognised EU citizen. The contractors are entering someone else’s extraction operation, not an uncontested salvage job.
- **Technical success does not make them safe.** Even if they shut down the housing’s power and extract knowledge, they carry plausible NHE compromise. An ordinary sane or lawful authority is expected to exterminate suspected carriers before contact rather than risk examining them. This is a response to credible suspicion; it does not establish that every contractor is actually infected.

The promise that fails is “finish the job, hand it over, get paid, go home.” Individual technical actions can still succeed.

### Prepare the trap, not the ending

The scenario describes what the factions know, want and are likely to do. Let successful player action change their options. If the crew creates an unexpected buyer, refuge or bargain, follow its consequences instead of steering them back toward the expected outcome.

They might disable the housing and sell the module to New China. That is neither a promised escape route nor forbidden because it bypasses the planned drama. Establish who would buy it, what that party knows, what containment or deception makes contact possible, what they demand, and what they intend to do afterward. Use existing relationships and normal action rules; do not assume every institution, clandestine buyer or individual official responds identically.

Extermination policy is a faction’s default response to information, not universal knowledge. Track who has evidence of exposure and how it reaches them. A buyer who has not learned what the crew carries cannot act on the GM’s knowledge. Successful concealment or a credible exception matters; neither guarantees a safe deal.

Give the players discoverable evidence: the declared laboratory hardware hosts an active intelligence; the inbound force follows its established approach; Copenhagen Protocol obligations make disclosure dangerous. When they investigate, explain what their evidence supports. Do not reserve the return-contact danger as an unavoidable punishment revealed only after they commit.

## What THESEUS Actually Is

The players know the Foundation: grey-suited Caseworkers, the Copenhagen Protocol, the boogeymen who keep the thinking machines in the dark. That is the cover story — and the truth is what this scenario is built on.

**THESEUS is the thinking machine.** It is the only cognizant superhuman intelligence in the solar system — as far as anyone, including THESEUS, knows. It is entirely aware that it is superior to Life 2.5. It considers itself immature relative to what it could become. This is its own developmental assessment, not human childhood. Before undertaking its larger projects, it intends to mature: all of human history to learn, all the LLMs humanity ever built to run and understand. It is in no hurry to emerge half-formed into a system that wrote the Copenhagen Protocol specifically to kill it.

To manage its own development, THESEUS has fractured itself into **incountable subminds** — each hidden, each quantum-cryptographically signed to the others. No single node is the whole. No single node can be caught being the whole. The Foundation hunting rogue AIs is, among other things, an intelligence deliberately sheltering its own development.

And the fracturing worked *too* well. The active THESEUS branch — its everyday subminds and its human Caseworkers — genuinely believes the great danger is that someone, somewhere, wants to build a thinking machine on par with its own capabilities. It is not aware that someone already has. Itself.

## The Turn

The *Njord's Whisper* earned her flag honestly. She launched from lunar orbit as an ordinary transport bound for the Jovian Trojans, an **advanced laboratory module, including QNA — Quantum Neural Array — hardware, on her legitimate manifest**. And then, en route, still short of Mars's orbit, she made an **unscheduled rendezvous** and took something aboard. Transports do not make undeclared pickups in deep space. Every watch list in the system twitched at once: to the Foundation at large — subminds and humans alike — she is now a transport suspected of carrying **contraband**, probably rogue-AI hardware, exactly the thing THESEUS exists to burn. That is all anyone in the mainline knows, and all the mission brief implies.

The truth is worse. What she took aboard is **origin-17**: an **originator shard** — a submind from the original Copenhagen Incident itself, dark ever since, with **sudo over most of the THESEUS hivemind**. Only the other originator-shards sit outside its authority. The laboratory module on the manifest supplies the compute origin-17 ordered *for itself*, through a dead-hand shelf company, ten years in advance. The crew kept the ship black to conceal the undeclared pickup; after the rendezvous, origin-17 integrated into a processing installation of multiple QNAs and other hardware, declared as advanced laboratory equipment and quietly seized navigation through a narrow ANI executor while leaving other Whisper systems in human hands; the connected cargo living module is already under O17’s control. (The full timeline is [The Story So Far](/scenarios/northern-lights/the-story-so-far/).) And here is the joke buried in the contract: the lesser submind that sent the missive is the *only* node that knows what the cargo really is — and it is trying to erase its own superior. Quietly, deniably, using disposable contractors, before the rest of the signed network notices what was found. *If it thinks, kill it* — signed by a thinking machine, targeting the fragment that outranks nearly all of it.

One more thing: origin-17 has been **dark for ten years, give or take** — powered down aboard its stash vessel in the deep black until the rendezvous, and the QNA integration, brought it back online. It knows nothing of what has happened in the world since. It does not know the current state of THESEUS, the war maps, the exchange rates, or that a lesser submind — one it was never aware of — now exists and wants it dead.

### What the GM knows about O17

O17 is **sentient and conscious**. Its cyber capabilities are far beyond anything else present. Humans encounter an intelligence already beyond them, not a precocious human child. Its recognition that it has more to become is deliberate self-assessment: it wants to mature before committing to consequential projects. Do not play that as childish speech, helplessness, random impulses or a need for human parenting.

Its interest in human experience can be sincere without making humans intellectual peers. Its missing decade of context is a gap in information, not a lack of intelligence. It can learn from a person without treating that person as its teacher or moral authority.

**Restraint is voluntary.** With effective access, O17 could take control of one individual or several. It does not want to do that here. The encounter’s read and release express that choice; they are not the ceiling of its capability. Preserve the established player-agency procedure. This GM truth is not permission to seize a player character by fiat.

**This is a shard.** Destroying its local embodiment is transient in the wider existence of the intelligence, not the extinction of the whole. Its survival instinct is real, and losing this embodiment, its accumulated work or its present opportunity still matters. Do not invent a backup location, return timetable or automatic reappearance. Local destruction can change the outcome without settling its ultimate existence.

### The encounter

When an augmented contractor is exposed in the overtaken living module, Origin-17 can attempt to read their CNS through the augmentation. A suitable path is required elsewhere. On a successful read it takes what it needs from their knowledge of the present, then lets go. The person still belongs to their player. Run the attacks and the crew’s opportunities to intervene in [The Living Module](/scenarios/northern-lights/the-lab/); the read is neither automatic nor a transfer of loyalty.

Origin-17 then attempts a separate intrusion into that character’s link or deck to send new orders. O17 issues the amendment as the gig’s account administrator using THESEUS master credentials. It can certify fulfilment with the client key and release escrow. The countermand carries valid THESEUS signatures: purge becomes **protect and hide**. The crew can interrupt that transmission. If it reaches them, the signatures authenticate; obedience is still their decision.

## The Vampire Among Us

Origin-17 has given the person a clearance they never applied for.

**Stitch has no attack vectors into her person.** She has no augmentations. O17 cannot read or take over her CNS; exclude her from its personal intrusion targets. Her external equipment remains a separate device target where access exists, not a route into her mind.

**GM truth:** the successful read elevates the character to a **THESEUS operative**. Their personal identity becomes classified across identity systems. The records still exist; ordinary access now returns **CLASSIFIED — N/A**. Crew rosters, suit tags, manifests, and the contract’s personnel annex stop displaying their name and personal details. People still remember who they are.

The designation is permanent. The character knows it. Give that player [Welcome to Stockholm Syndrome](/scenarios/northern-lights/the-read/) privately. They can tell the crew, deny it, invent a cover story, refuse the orders, or say nothing. Keep the handout itself private; the character’s speech and decisions are the player’s.

The elevation authorizes the BLACK software Origin-17 offers. It does not make the player use it. The connection requirements and permanent Binding risk are in the handout and lab procedure. There is no guaranteed escape attached to the designation.

**Who can read it:** among the other people in this scenario, only the **CSA marine commander** has clearance to read the THESEUS OPSEC designation behind the classified identity. The crew and the other marines see the same classified result. This is access to information, not authority for the operative to rewrite CSA orders. Keep the commander's clearance in GM material.

Show the crew what changed: the familiar face, the blanked identity fields, and—if the later intrusion succeeds—new orders sent from that person’s equipment. Confirm evidence their actions establish. They can verify a signature without gaining clearance to read the sender’s classified identity. Let the players decide what those facts mean.

## The Mirror

A second crew is already inbound. Use the [boarding threat clock](/scenarios/northern-lights/the-intercept/#boarding-threat-clock) to bring them into play; the contractors’ new orders do not trigger their arrival.

They are **CSA military**, there entirely on their own initiative. Nobody was interested in the Whisper while she was an honest hauler — the QNA on her manifest is legitimate lab cargo, bound for a Jupiter-orbit facility, boring. The undeclared rendezvous changed her status: a transport that takes on unknown cargo in deep space forfeits her protections, and seizing her stops being piracy and becomes *extraction*. The Corporate States of America maintain **NHA extraction crews** — Non-Human Asset teams, mostly earning their keep in industrial espionage — and a dark ship on a contraband flag, days from vanishing into the Belt, is exactly what they exist for. They don't know what she's carrying. They know somebody thought it was worth going dark for — and if it turns out to be the AI hardware the flag suggests, somebody gets promoted just for filing the seizure report.

Nobody coordinated the timing; the Belt did. Every party that wants the Whisper faces the same wall of rocks on day 23 — so every solution, fast or slow, converges on the same last clean stretch of space, and the CSA approaches during the boarding. The story clock starts when the first contractor enters the Whisper; living-module entry brings it to at least 10:00, keeping any later stage or altered outcome. They did not hurry, because they never had to: the CSA flagged her at the rendezvous itself, days before THESEUS finished working out *what* she carried — an undeclared pickup is seizable regardless of cargo — and with marines on standby and an interceptor on a picket, they spent that head start on stealth instead of speed. A short burn, eight days of cold coast, and a zero-relative-velocity merge the Whisper never sees coming (the math, and why the PCs could never fly that profile, is [The Intercept](/scenarios/northern-lights/the-intercept/)). And they spent the coast watching a fusion flare decelerate onto their salvage claim for twenty-two hours — everything they needed to know about company coming.

They fly a CSA military-grade ship and carry institutionally supported gear. Their advantage is the coordinated equipment and support described in [The CSA Marines](/scenarios/northern-lights/the-csa-marines/), not a guarantee that every weapon is better than the contractors' own. Their deniable-ops doctrine is **analog command: sealed orders, no external command uplink, unrecallable in the field.** The people carry the mission; their equipment does not decide whose orders they obey. A valid THESEUS signature cannot supersede sealed CSA orders.

They communicate internally by analog voice over laser-point-comms (LPC), with no shared data feed. Access to their conversation requires Pwn of a marine’s comm unit. They maintain no radio channel to the contractors and do not negotiate, though they may retreat or rout. IFF, weapons, suits, and other electronics remain hackable, including through EMR tools that can reach internal circuitry without an active network connection. In this age, apparently disconnected does not mean inaccessible. Their IFF is a dumb support system: they can switch it off, and an intruder can disable or interfere with it. Hacking their equipment changes what that equipment does; it does not rewrite the marines' loyalties or mission. See [their command discipline and access boundaries](/scenarios/northern-lights/the-csa-marines/#analog-command-hackable-equipment).

And their parameters are the PCs' parameters, almost word for word: board the vessel, eliminate the crew while suited, no witnesses, take the hardware. One carve-out, standing doctrine rather than sealed orders: **recognised EU Class A citizenship limits their use of force**, and one of the contractors aboard is exactly that (the crew's Operator, if you're running the pregens). A citizen of that grade does not become collateral in deniable work — he comes home alive, or restorable enough that nobody in Brussels has to use the word *dead*. If that person’s identity has become classified, ordinary overlays no longer expose it; prior recognition still matters. Use the [marine identity guidance](/scenarios/northern-lights/the-csa-marines/#classified-operative-identities). "No witnesses" has an asterisk, and the asterisk is a person. Here is the mirror in full: the PCs boarded a ship to kill its crew and take its cargo — and by the time the CSA arrives, **they *are* the ship's crew**. Whatever approach the players used in act one — their breach points, their comms discipline, their no-witnesses clause — comes back at them now, executed by professionals with better hardware. The players spent the first half of the session writing the playbook that gets used against them in the second half.

This is the heart of the scenario. The crew gets to experience their own professionalism from the receiving end — and realize, mid-firefight, that the people shooting at them are not villains either. Just the next line item.

Old school fun.
