# Northern Lights: would I feel ready to host this?

**I want to run this premise. I would not invite people to play the published package yet.** The switch from boarding a civilian ship to defending it is strong, and the surviving spacers give my players' treatment of people a useful payoff. But the promise that I can run it straight from the pages gets ahead of what those pages provide. I would be finishing encounters, reconciling conflicting instructions, and editing player materials before hosting.

This is a synthetic reader's response, not a playtest result. My assumed persona has some familiarity with tabletop RPGs, is new to running FREE//FALL, and wants to host the advertised three-to-five-hour session for three to five friends. I am willing to learn the rules and improvise responses to players. I do not expect to design the demo's missing procedures.

I read all 20 Northern Lights Markdown pages, including the five pregens, both handouts, opposition, vessels, and backstory. I followed the relevant material through the seven core-rulebook pages, the SRD, and the catalog entries needed to check equipment and Binding. The project's web-native vision informs the usability comments. This is a review of the local source revision above; it does not claim browser testing or production-site verification.

The first five findings affect my ability to host confidently. The five pet peeves afterward are smaller, subjective preparation frustrations. Proposed changes describe what would help this reader; no scenario or rules changes are made by this report.

## Five major issues

### 1. The demo leaves me to write the climax and resolve the actual mission

**My reaction:** “I have learned how to get everyone into trouble. Where is the material that lets me finish the evening?”

The [overview](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/index.md#who-its-for) says “runs out of the box” and “no prep beyond reading the GM pages.” But [The Lab](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/the-lab.md#remaining-mechanical-work) explicitly leaves target TNs, defensive timing, shielding effects, extraction, isolation, and purge procedures unfinished. It also leaves the consequence of The Singularity reaching the host's Ghost capacity unresolved. That is a risk the player is asked to accept before I can explain its full consequences.

[The Second Boarding](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/the-second-boarding.md) stops at impact. The [marine reference](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/the-csa-marines.md#when-the-picture-breaks) still requires loadouts, defenses, reserves, withdrawal triggers, and an escape procedure. There is no subsequent encounter or ending page in the scenario manifest.

This is more than an open ending. Extracting the logs and purging the QNA are the original job; protecting it becomes the alternative job. I need to know what carrying out either decision requires. If the crew refuses the override, destroys the array, defeats the marines, or tries to leave with survivors, I am constructing the resolution myself. Generic action rules can resolve an attempt after I invent the problem; they do not supply the missing situation.

**What would help:** finish one complete playable route through the confrontation and aftermath, with concise branches for purge, protection, escape, and defeat. Define the costs players must choose between. Until then, describe the package as a partially playable draft on the overview, where I decide whether to host it.

### 2. The GM preface prepares me for a different reveal from the supplied handout

**My reaction:** “Did I miss a handout, or am I about to promise a player a power that no longer exists?”

In [The Vampire Among Us](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/preface.md#the-vampire-among-us), the affected character is erased from manifests, HUD tags, suit transponders, and the contract roster. Their identity becomes `CLASSIFIED — N/A`. The private handout supposedly grants a one-use personal guarantee of escape. The GM is told that their “only job” is never to confirm anything.

The actual [private handout](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/the-read.md) contains neither that escape guarantee nor the roster erasure. It provides reassurance about player control and an optional BLACK software suite with permanent Ghost Binding risk. [The Lab](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/the-lab.md#first-intrusion-read-the-person) describes a read followed by relinquished access, then a separate device intrusion to send the override. Its investigation guidance gives me evidence I can confirm about the device and transmission.

These are different ways of creating suspicion. One uses a visible identity deletion and a secret escape offer; the other uses an intrusive read, authenticated orders from compromised equipment, and dangerous assistance. The preface never labels its version obsolete. I could introduce the missing identity effect, hand over the current text, and spend the next ten minutes improvising why the promised secret is absent.

**What would help:** make the preface, encounter, and handouts describe the same sequence. Put the trigger, public signs, private information, and actual mechanical offer together in a short GM reference. Keep uncertainty about Origin-17's motives; remove uncertainty about which instructions I am meant to follow.

### 3. The clock promises consequences that the scene instructions then move around

**My reaction:** “Am I tracking their time, or timing the entrance to my dramatic reveal?”

[Contact](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/contact.md#reading-the-choices) sells Ghost as spending hours and risking the CSA arriving during boarding. Ram is fast enough to get the contractors aboard first. The approach and onboarding both ask me to record elapsed time. Those instructions encourage me to treat speed as a consequential player choice.

Then [The Intercept](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/the-intercept.md#the-two-clocks-merged) gives a contact-plus-six-hours arrival but calls that lead an approximate staging guide: set the merge so identification happens as the mission override verifies. [The Lab](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/the-lab.md#carry-it-forward) repeats that transition, including an alternative trigger if the override is blocked. That handles the blocked transmission well, but not a crew that spends a radically different amount of time reaching the lab.

Suppose they ram, bypass most of the defenders, and reach the modules quickly. Or they spend hours negotiating and repairing. I cannot both keep the arrival attached to the lab reveal and preserve the advertised risk of the marines catching them earlier. No approach durations or overrun procedure settle the choice. Stealth still earns concealment and sensors can buy warning; those are real consequences, but they do not resolve the clock contradiction.

**What would help:** choose a clear procedure. Either use a fixed arrival with rough action durations and instructions for an early interception, or use an explicitly dramatic trigger and price the approaches through position and resources. I need to know which promises to make before the players choose.

### 4. I cannot give players their pregens without spoiling the scenario

**My reaction:** “The obvious character link tells its player what I am supposed to reveal later.”

The [overview](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/index.md#who-its-for) invites players to pick a ready-made contractor while warning them away from GM information. Each character page mixes the usable sheet with an unlabeled-by-audience “In this scenario” section.

[Vector](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/c-01-the-pilot.md#in-this-scenario) is told that a machine forges paperwork-perfect orders. [Root](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/c-03-the-wirehead.md#in-this-scenario) learns that the array borrows a crewmate's brain. [Vantage](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/c-02-the-operator.md#in-this-scenario) gets the second boarding and the marines' capture restriction explained. These are not merely personal hooks; they disclose encounters and hidden mechanisms that other pages build around discovery.

If I obey the overview's blanket GM-only warning, I still have no clean player-facing character packet to send. If I send the character pages, I spoil several reveals. Having to cut and paste five bespoke sheets is significant unadvertised preparation for an introductory scenario. The problem does not require secret URLs or access controls: it requires a clearly shareable piece of content.

**What would help:** provide a player-facing version containing identity, known relationships, equipment, usable rules, and starting resources. Put future scene guidance and hidden advantages in a separate GM section or page, with an explicit sharing boundary.

### 5. The starting equipment leaves basic character legality for me to reconcile

**My reaction:** “Before teaching Binding, I need to repair the demo's own example of it.”

Two cross-checks stopped me:

- [Vantage's sheet](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/c-02-the-operator.md#sheet) includes a Combat Deck in both standard configurations but no Neural Link. The [catalog deck](../../../content/gear/items/utility/combat-deck.md) explicitly requires a Neural Link; the [link](../../../content/gear/items/augmentations/neural-link.md) binds Ghost 3 and supplies its own Load Bearing. The Wraith has a Ghostlink Interface, but neither the sheet nor [frame entry](../../../content/gear/items/exos/wraith-infiltration-frame.md) says it substitutes for the required cybernetic implant. I cannot tell whether the deck is usable, an implant is missing, or a scenario exception is intended. Adding the implant also changes the advertised resource picture.
- [Root](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/c-03-the-wirehead.md#sheet) has Initial Body 6 and Body 5 bound into installed augmentations, leaving Body 1. [The Vessel](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/the-vessel.md#effect) binds another Body 2 for every occupant under thrust. Taken literally, the opening transit gives Root a maximum Current Body of `6 − 5 − 2 = −1`. The [Binding rules](../../../content/core-rulebook/chapters/03-core-rules.md#gear-augmentations--binding-the-cost-of-capability) make costs cumulative but do not explain what I should do with this starting state. The transit is mostly a montage, so this need not prevent the story opening, but the vessel specifically tells me to make its cost felt. I need an explicit transit configuration or exception rather than silently ignoring it.

These are not complaints that every capability needs a separate subsystem. They are prerequisite and resource questions in a game whose main teaching promise is that equipment has concrete costs. The sheets' confident arithmetic encourages me to trust them precisely where I need cross-page reconciliation.

**What would help:** validate each pregen in its actual starting configuration, including the shared vessel and required interfaces. Show the transition from transit to boarding, and state any scenario exceptions where the player uses them.

## Five pet peeves

### 1. References send me looking for appendices the navigation does not name

[Contact](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/contact.md) repeatedly points to “Appendix A.02” and “Appendix B.01”; the [preface](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/preface.md) adds A.01. The [manifest](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/index.md) instead gives me named Backstory and Gear pages. I can work out which is which, but this is unnecessary detective work in a web-native game. Link “The Intercept” and “The Vessel” directly whenever I need them.

### 2. The assistant's percentages look actionable but give me no usable roll

[Contact](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/contact.md#the-assistants-read) gives detection risks of `0.03%` and `8.12%`, then discusses a bad or rare detection roll. The [action rules](../../../content/core-rulebook/chapters/03-core-rules.md#action-resolution-making-your-move) use d20 pools and TNs; the scene supplies no translation. The decimals are a good characterization of an overconfident assistant. Label them as its in-fiction estimates and give the GM the actual procedure, or say detection is adjudicated narratively. Otherwise I pause to wonder whether I need percentile dice with extra decimal places.

### 3. I get orbital mathematics before I get a usable sketch of the ship

[The Intercept](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/the-intercept.md) carefully explains burns and velocities. Meanwhile, [Onboarding](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/onboarding.md#opening-by-entry), [The Approach](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/the-approach.md#fields-of-fire), and [The Lab](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/the-lab.md#the-route-through-gm) depend on compartments, sightlines, hatches, and a twenty-centimetre gap. There is useful positional prose, but no supplied diagram in the scenario package to orient me quickly. A small linked compartment schematic, with aft/forward, pressure boundaries, and entry points, would save me more preparation than another explanation of the intercept. I do not need a gridded battle map.

### 4. “Current Pool” makes me do a double take, and “sixteen points” makes me check the sums

The pregens label their remaining Body/Mind/Ghost values “Current Pool,” while the most important pool I teach is the separate five-die Action Pool. The distinction is recoverable from the rules, but the sheets would be clearer with “Available Attributes,” followed by Action Pool and Harm Slots. Then [Vantage's sheet](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/c-02-the-operator.md#sheet) calls his underlying capacity “the same sixteen points of human” despite the listed `12 + 8 + 6 = 26`. The supplied calculations use the actual scores; this is a prose error. It still sends me back through arithmetic I thought I had finished checking.

### 5. The prose keeps telling me how perfectly the theme will land

The [preface](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/preface.md#the-mirror) explains the mirror at length; [Contact](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/contact.md#the-assistants-read) explains how the assistant's reassurance should age; [Vector's guidance](../../../content/scenarios/northern-lights/c-01-the-pilot.md#in-this-scenario) assures me that the player will figure things out “at the exact right moment.” “Check your seals” also closes the overview and each of the first three scenes. Some repetition establishes a voice. Here I start feeling responsible for delivering the author's predicted reactions. Give me observable cues, likely questions, and consequences, then leave room for my group's response.
